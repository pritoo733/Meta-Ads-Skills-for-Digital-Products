# Creative and product-page conversion

Develop original ad concepts that make the right buyer understand the product and the next step. Strong creative earns a qualified action by showing a relevant use case, mechanism, experience, or evidence—not by making the largest promise.

## Build an angle from evidence

For each concept, fill this chain:

1. **Buyer and moment:** who is this for, and what is happening when the problem matters?
2. **Insight:** what do customer interviews, support tickets, reviews, search queries, or product usage actually say?
3. **Tension:** what workaround, trade-off, or uncertainty can be stated truthfully?
4. **Mechanism/demo:** what can the product show or make easier? Use a real product capture or clearly labeled prototype.
5. **Proof:** which approved evidence supports the claim? If none, use a product fact, transparent demo, or hypothesis rather than a result claim.
6. **Objection:** what question about fit, price, compatibility, effort, or outcome should be answered before the click?
7. **Action and destination:** what should the buyer do, and does the landing page continue the same promise and terms?

Do not copy another advertiser's script, branded visual identity, or proprietary framing. Create distinct hypotheses, not minor rewrites of one hook.

## Format execution

- **Image:** one clear claim or use case, legible product evidence at feed scale, minimal necessary text, and an obvious relation to the offer.
- **Short video:** show the problem context or result early, demonstrate one product action, add captions, keep the message understandable without sound, and use a direct call to action.
- **Carousel or sequence:** use only when the steps, comparison, or feature set benefit from ordered frames; each frame should add information.
- **Creator/customer content:** obtain rights and permission; disclose material relationships where required; do not fabricate testimonial language or make the customer imply unsupported outcomes.

Match the ad to the destination's first screen, offer, price/terms, product compatibility, refund policy, and checkout. Avoid surprise pricing or a different promise after the click.

## Creative test matrix

Build creative variety at the concept level: different buyer motivations, use cases, mechanisms, objections, or kinds of proof. A new crop, opening line, or caption can be useful production variation, but it is not automatically a new strategic hypothesis. Meta Blueprint recommends diversification by concept and format; adapt that principle to the budget and the seller's ability to read the result.

Digital products can be hard to picture before purchase. Prefer a truthful product demonstration: show the SaaS workflow, a template in use, a plugin installation or output, a course excerpt, a download preview, or a community experience. If the real interface or customer material cannot be shown, use a clearly labeled mockup and do not present it as a real customer result.

Change the primary concept dimension deliberately and keep the offer, destination, and event stable when practical. A native creative-testing feature or a properly designed experiment may help compare variants when available; verify its current behavior in the account. Otherwise, compare delivery as an observational read, note unequal spend and audience overlap, and avoid claiming causality. Do not flood a small budget with more ads than it can meaningfully evaluate.

Evaluate delivery, attention, click quality, page conversion, purchases, refunds, activation, and retention as relevant. Use engagement metrics to diagnose a mechanism, not as a substitute for the business result.

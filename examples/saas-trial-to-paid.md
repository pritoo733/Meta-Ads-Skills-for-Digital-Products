# Illustrative example: SaaS trial to paid

**Fictional arithmetic only. These figures are not benchmarks or a forecast.**

## Situation

A fictional SaaS seller wants more paid customers. It has a 14-day trial, tracks account creation and a meaningful first-project activation event, and receives payment records from its billing system. Its current paid cohort is too small to estimate long-term retention confidently.

## Plan

1. Define the buyer and use case; confirm that the ad and landing page show the same supported product workflow.
2. Map signup → first-project activation → paid conversion → first renewal. Keep signup as a diagnostic metric and paid customer as the business outcome.
3. Use observed trial activation and paid conversion for the cohort, but keep later renewal contribution pending until the period matures.
4. Check browser/server event consistency, consent, deduplication, and billing-system paid status before trusting the purchase signal.
5. Test one buyer/problem hypothesis in creative while holding offer and destination stable where practical.
6. Set a spend cap from the seller's allowable acquisition cost and cash tolerance. If the cap cannot answer whether the cohort reaches activation or payment, simplify the test or collect evidence first.

## Decision readout structure

Report spend, paid customers, media CAC, contribution horizon, activated trials, paid conversion cohort maturity, and unresolved renewal evidence. A click or signup improvement alone does not justify scale. Compare the observed CAC to the seller's allowable CAC only when cohort and cost definitions match.
